The Complete Guide to Sant Andreu de Palomar

There are neighbourhoods of Barcelona, and there are villages of Barcelona. Sant Andreu de Palomar belongs to the second category — a historic core with over a thousand years of documented history, in the city's District 9.

Walking along Carrer Gran, around Plaça del Mercadal or through the old town's side streets, you notice something hard to define but unmistakable: people know each other. Neighbours greet each other by name. Century-old shops coexist with new ones. The squares feel like real life, not a tourist postcard.

Sant Andreu de Palomar has over a thousand years of documented history. It was an independent village until 1897, when a decree annexed it to Barcelona against the will of its inhabitants. That moment was never forgotten. During the Festa Major on 30 November, inside the church a banner makes it clear: «SANT ANDREU NO ÉS BARCELONA».

This guide is for lifelong locals who want to know their neighbourhood better, for newcomers trying to understand where they've landed, and for anyone who believes Barcelona is much more than the Sagrada Família and Las Ramblas.

Sant Andreu at a glance

First mention: 992 · Area: 174 ha · Residents: ~55,000 · Forced annexation: 1897 · Associations: 267+

Location & boundaries

Historic core of the Sant Andreu District (no. 9). Bordered to the north by Montcada i Reixac, east by Meridiana, south by La Sagrera, and west by Riera d'Horta.

Getting there

Metro L1: Sant Andreu, Fabra i Puig. Metro L9/L10: Onze de Setembre. Commuter rail: Sant Andreu Arenal. Bus: H6, V23, 40, 62, 73, 192.

Main Festival

Around 30 November, feast of Saint Andrew the Apostle. The Esclat Andreuenc marks the start.

The District

Seven neighbourhoods: Sant Andreu de Palomar, La Sagrera, El Bon Pastor, Baró de Viver, Congrés i els Indians, Navas and La Trinitat Vella. HQ: Plaça d'Orfila, 1.

The name: "de Palomar", not "del Palomar"

The historically correct form is Sant Andreu de Palomar, without the article. It comes from the church of Sant Andreu (St Andrew) at the place called Palomar, documented in 992 and joined in a 1034 text. The common variant Sant Andreu del Palomar (with "del") is widespread, but would literally mean "St Andrew's dovecote". In Spanish: San Andrés de Palomar. Locals campaign for the official article-free name.
